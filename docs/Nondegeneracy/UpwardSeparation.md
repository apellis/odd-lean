# Bounded cyclotomic separation for the upward route

Research toward Brundan–Ellis, *Super Kac–Moody 2-categories*, §8,
Nondegeneracy Conjecture; tracking issue #345. This note supplies a mathematical
bounded-separation argument. It does **not** construct an SKM action or claim
that the conjecture is proved. The refined straightening statement below is not
yet a Lean theorem.

## A parameter-independent bound

Fix the strand number `n` and put `L = n(n−1)/2`. Work in the affine quiver-Hecke
supercategory of the repository, with arbitrary permitted scalar parameters.
Use its independently proved affine PBW basis (`QuiverHecke.basisNF`), written
`x^a τ_w`. Every reduced permutation word has at most `L` crossings. Neither
symmetrizability nor a homogeneous Cartan grading is needed here.

For a positioned word `g`, define its integer score by

`s(g) = number of dots − number of crossings`.

**Refined straightening lemma.** A word of score `s` is a finite linear
combination of PBW words `x^a τ_w` satisfying `|a| − length(w) ≥ s`.

Proof: refine the existing terminating straightening proof, retaining this
inequality at each local replacement. Distant interchanges and dot sorting
preserve the score. A dot-crossing interchange has score zero on both leading
terms and its identity correction also has score zero. A quadratic crossing
replacement has input score −2; every nonzero correction is a polynomial in
dots and hence has score at least zero. A braid replacement has input score −3;
the leading term has the same score and every correction is a polynomial in
dots. These are the actual polynomial expressions in `PositionalRelations`,
including their nonhomogeneous scalar terms. Context adds its own score to
every summand. Thus every local step used in Coxeter reduction and dot sorting
preserves the required lower bound. The termination proof already used for
`span_normalForms` applies unchanged. This proof is about a chosen terminating
expansion, and does not assert that arbitrary reverse relation applications
preserve the score.

For every sequence of colours `ν`, choose an exponent `N_ν ≥ N`. Let `J_N` be
the two-sided categorical ideal generated by `x_1^(N_ν) 1_ν` in strand number
`n` (for `n=0` there is no such generator). Let `V_d` be the span of PBW words
having at most `d` dots.

**Bounded separation theorem.** If `N > d + 2L`, the quotient by `J_N` is
injective on `V_d`.

Proof: an ideal element is a finite sum of terms `a x_1^(N_ν) b`. First expand
`a` and `b` in affine PBW bases. This step is essential: the unreduced crossing
counts of arbitrary multipliers have no uniform bound. Each resulting
multiplier has score at least `−L`. Their concatenation with the cyclotomic
generator therefore has score at least `N−2L`. Refined straightening shows
that every PBW term in this ideal element satisfies

`|a| − length(w) ≥ N−2L`, hence `|a| ≥ N−2L`.

Consequently `J_N` is contained in the span of the PBW labels with at least
`N−2L` dots. These labels are disjoint from those of `V_d`. Affine PBW linear
independence gives `J_N ∩ V_d = 0`, over any allowed ground ring, including
rings with zero divisors. There is no division by scalars in this argument.

Taking `N` arbitrarily large separates every finite affine PBW combination.
This conclusion uses affine QH independence only, never independence of its
image in SKM.

## Full coefficient rings and genuinely deformed quotients

The same proof works with a spectator superalgebra `A`. Here the super tensor
product uses the multiplication
`(a ⊗ h)(b ⊗ g) = (−1)^(|h||b|) ab ⊗ hg` on homogeneous elements.
Every `δ_i` is a distinct odd generator, with square zero and
`δ_i δ_j = −δ_j δ_i`. All elementary coefficient generators are even.
Thus an odd coefficient anticommutes with every odd dot or odd crossing,
and commutes with every even generator. The underlying tensor product is
taken over the original even commutative ground ring, with the grading of
the QH generators from its presentation. Its underlying module is a direct
sum of copies of `A` by the already proved affine PBW decomposition;
tensoring a free PBW decomposition does not require a flatness hypothesis.
Koszul signs are units and do not identify different exterior generators.
For `A = SYM`, the argument therefore keeps `δ_i δ_j`, rather than replacing
all odd coefficients by zero or one common odd parameter. The repository's
current QH theorem is over an even commutative ground ring; the super tensor
extension and its sign convention must still be implemented explicitly.

There is also a useful weighted extension. Suppose the coefficient algebra
has a nonnegative grading. Give a coefficient monomial its weight and replace
the score by

`coefficient weight + dot count − crossing count`.

If every term in every cyclotomic generator has coefficient weight plus dot
count at least `N`, the same ideal argument proves injectivity on total-weight
at-most-`d` PBW combinations for `N > d + 2L`. The generators need not be pure
powers. In a monic deformation, a coefficient multiplying `x^(N−j)` must
have weight at least `j`; assigning a constant coefficient weight zero would
invalidate the argument. The QH structural scalars retain weight zero.

Additional relations in the coefficient ring are harmless in bounded weight
only if their entire ideal is supported in sufficiently high weight. A
nonnegative grading with homogeneous generators of weights tending to infinity
is one sufficient condition. This is the relevant test for finite-rank
Grassmannian deformation rings, not merely whether each ring individually
contains an exterior symbol. For the abstract `SYM`, natural weights are
`weight(e_i,r)=r+1` at even colours, `2(r+1)` at odd colours, and
`weight(δ_i)=1`. Finite expressions have finite weight. An independent
polynomial-times-exterior basis construction can verify that high-weight
relations cannot kill a bounded combination. This coefficient-basis step does
not involve any SKM Hom space.

There is a concrete coefficient check for the ELV deformation presentation
([arXiv:2203.14153v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.14153v2), §3.5, (3.24)).
In positive cyclotomic degree `n`, put `m=floor(n/2)`. Its coefficient algebra has even
generators `χ_1,...,χ_m` and odd `d`, with `d²=0` and, when `n` is even,
the additional relation `d χ_m=0`. With `weight(χ_r)=2r` and `weight(d)=1`,
use the one-based notation `e_r = SYM.e i (r−1)` for `r≥1` in this paragraph.
The map from one-colour abstract `SYM` sends `e_r` to `χ_r` for `r≤m`,
`e_r` to zero for `r>m`, and `δ` to `d`. Its kernel is generated by the
omitted elementary generators and, in even degree, `δ e_m`. The smallest
weight of these generators is `n+1`. Thus this quotient is injective in
weights at most `n`, including the odd coefficient. For several colours,
the super tensor product retains the products of their distinct odd
generators; its kernel has the same lower bound using the smallest degree.
This follows directly from the polynomial-times-exterior monomial basis.

The source cyclotomic polynomial has
`c_(2r)=χ_r`, `c_(2r+1)=dχ_r` and
`a^n(t)=Σ_(l=0)^n (-1)^l t^l c_(n-l)`.
Every term has total weight `n` if the odd dot `t` has weight one.
Every term also has parity `n mod 2`, since `c_j` has parity `j mod 2`.
Consequently both its cyclotomic relations and its coefficient-kernel
relations satisfy the weighted support criterion. This supplies a concrete
deformed *algebraic* detector in growing rank, subject to implementing the
supertensor PBW extension. It does not supply the SKM action or identify its
normalized bubbles with this particular coefficient map.

The source presentation just quoted is rank one. The extension here to a
formal multicolour quotient is the argument of this note: start with the
supertensor coefficient algebra over all colours and the independent affine
QH algebra; impose each colour's coefficient relations, and at the first
strand of every colour sequence impose the homogeneous cyclotomic polynomial
belonging to that strand's colour. Before quotienting,
the tensor-product module is coefficientwise PBW by the preceding explicit
construction. After quotienting, the weighted ideal-support proof establishes
bounded injectivity; it does not assume freeness of the entire quotient.
This construction is not a multicolour SKM action supplied by the rank-one
source. We use this source presentation over `Q` for the pilot; any use over a broader
ground ring here refers to this explicitly defined algebraic presentation,
not an extension of the source's categorical theorem.

**Limitation:** a unit constant term has weight zero and violates the
hypothesis. The theorem does not establish separation for the invertible-dot
second factor. Nor does `SYM.unitCutoff` establish that the abstract
specialization occurs as normalized bubble evaluation in such a factor.
The separate action investigation reports an even-degree deformation
constraint relating the odd parameter to the top elementary coefficient;
making the latter invertible can kill the former. The weighted nilpotent
factor is a possible way to retain odd parameters, but requires checking the
actual coefficient-ring presentation and bubble normalization.

## What remains for upward SKM independence

For a finite proposed relation, one needs models with all of the following
properties simultaneously:

1. An action of the complete SKM presentation, including coherent mates and
   all required inversion relations.
2. The upward affine generators act through the cyclotomic algebra just
   analyzed, or through a test subquotient with the same bounded detection.
3. Normalized abstract `SYM` coefficients evaluate injectively on the finite
   coefficient span under consideration, retaining mixed exterior products.
4. On the chosen test object, the combination of coefficient evaluation and
   upward action is the coefficientwise PBW map (possibly after an explicitly
   proved invertible triangular change of basis).

Given these inputs, apply the action to the proposed relation, evaluate on
the regular-module unit/test vector, and use bounded separation. The PBW
coefficients vanish in the model; coefficient detection then makes the
original `SYM` coefficients vanish. Merely proving coefficient detection and
operator detection in two unrelated factors does not supply item 4.

## Lean implementation boundary

Existing inputs are `QuiverHecke.basisNF`, the explicit local relation
expansions in `PositionalRelations`, and the terminating proof in `Spanning`.
`Straighten.Filt` and `Low` remember crossing counts only. Their general
membership statements cannot recover a lower bound on dots. However, the
existing *local* correction statements land in `Filt 0`, where every
representative has nonnegative score. This permits reuse of the local
relations without duplicating their polynomial expansions.

`OddMath/QuiverHecke/WeightedSeparation.lean` now establishes this local
reduction, score control under arbitrary contexts, and the joint
`BoundedScore` filtration recording crossing bounds and score bounds on the
same representatives. It also proves `weighted_approx_step` and
`weighted_braid_move`: arbitrary Coxeter braid equivalences have unit leading
coefficient with corrections of strictly fewer crossings and the required
score lower bound. Its scoped Lean check passed with warnings as errors.
The effective `positionedScore` ignores out-of-range positioned generators:
the repository interprets these as empty layers, so counting an invalid dot
would otherwise give arbitrarily high score to the identity. On valid words
it agrees with the stated dot-minus-crossing score.

Weighted dot transport and the final simultaneous refinement of the terminating
`Spanning` induction remain outstanding. Separate membership in a crossing span and a score span
is insufficient: the needed induction uses their joint generating set.
After this refinement, the ideal and disjoint-support arguments are
finite-sum and basis-coordinate arguments. Neither the bounded quotient
theorem nor the weighted ELV extension is currently a Lean theorem.

## A concrete coupling target for reservoir and spectral factors

The nilpotent-reservoir/nonzero-probe proposal is the earlier split-factor
plan with a significant correction: the odd bubble coefficients must be
detected in the reservoir, rather than inferred from an invertible-dot
factor in which the chosen source deformation kills them. Beyond that
correction, a list of reservoir and probe factors is not a new construction.

A useful next lemma is an explicit normalized-bubble transfer formula on
the coupled reservoir test object. After localization at the required
spectral separation determinants, seek

`β_coupled(δ_i) = u_i δ_res,i`,

`β_coupled(e_i,r) = v_i,r e_res,i,r + P_i,r`,

where the coefficients `u_i` and `v_i,r` are even units in the fixed
probe/base algebra (independent of the reservoir generators), and `P_i,r` involves
only lower-index reservoir elementary generators, exterior generators, and
probe parameters. The indexing must be a single well-founded triangular
order if different colours mix. These are proposed formulas to prove from
the chosen cups and caps, not formulas presently established. For fixed
finite coefficients they give an invertible triangular substitution in the
universal reservoir algebra; sufficiently large finite reservoir quotients
then detect the substituted finite span by the preceding weight bound.
The distinct exterior variables and unit coefficients explicitly retain
`δ_i δ_j`.

The same coupling lemma must identify the tested upward operator coordinates
with coefficientwise affine PBW coordinates (or a triangular unit-diagonal
variant). A block equivalence of upward algebras alone cannot justify the
bubble formulas: it must intertwine the selected adjunction and trace maps.
This is the concrete interface needed to combine the two independent
detectors, and a stronger next target than checking an additional isolated
rank-one relation.
