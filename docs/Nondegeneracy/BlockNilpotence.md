# Nilpotent closed-loop coefficients

This conditional algebraic argument concerns the block-inversion step in the Nondegeneracy Conjecture of Brundan–Ellis, *Super Kac–Moody 2-categories*. It is not yet formalized in Lean. The signed comparison with the SKM operators remains to be proved; no action or nondegeneracy theorem is asserted.

## Setup

Work in an additive k-linear category of superfunctors on one fixed first-factor content component. Let R=End(Id). Assume R is supercommutative and its homogeneous odd elements square to zero. For the intended factor this must follow from its actual bubble relations, not just supercommutativity in characteristic two.

In the existing SKM presentation, `Scalars.two_isUnit` already supplies invertibility of 2 whenever an odd colour exists. Thus supercommutativity does imply square-zero for odd coefficients in that application. The abstract argument below states square-zero explicitly rather than imposing invertibility of 2 on every coefficient ring.

Let X,Y be endofunctors, x:X→X and y:Y→Y uniformly nilpotent dot maps on this component, sigma:X→Y, u:Id→Y and v:X→Id. Write u_s=y^s u and v_s=v x^s. Normalize the actual mated slide to

    sigma x + y sigma = u v.                        (S)

For the even-colour minus convention replace y by -y and adjust signed u_s. In a superfunctor application all displayed direct summands have their prescribed Pi shifts. The unshifted matrix equations below must be checked against those comparisons; that check is NOT assumed completed.

The uniform nilpotence is componentwise: a fixed cyclotomic content has a uniform algebra-element bound on each dot. No uniform bound across the entire direct sum over contents is required.

For a>0 suppose the actual factor inversion is

    P=[sigma,u_0,...,u_(a-1)] : X ⊕ Id^a → Y,
    P^-1=(r,t_0,...,t_(a-1)).

Thus r sigma=1, r u_j=0, t_j sigma=0 and t_j u_l=delta_jl for j,l<a. We seek nilpotence of the finite family of closed coefficients v_s r u_t. Nilpotence of the ideal generated by all dots is NOT claimed.

## Companion calculation, negative-weight factor

Put w=r u_a, b_j=t_j u_a, and let J be the shift with J e_j=e_(j+1) for j<a-1 and J e_(a-1)=0. Equation (S) gives

    M=P^-1 y P = [ -x       w e_last^T ]
                   [ e_0 v   J+b e_last^T ].

Indeed r y sigma=-x, t y sigma=e_0 v, and y u_j=u_(j+1). The entire M is nilpotent, since y is.

Use an EVEN central formal parameter z. Both 1-zM and 1+zx have polynomial inverses (finite geometric series). Block Gaussian elimination therefore gives a polynomial inverse for the Schur matrix

    S(z)=1-zJ-[z b+z² e_0 C(z)]e_last^T,
    C(z)=v(1+zx)^-1 w = sum_s (-z)^s c_s,
    c_s=v x^s w.

Crucial point: S(z) is a matrix over R[z], and its inverse has entries in R[z] obtained by the categorical block inverse. No determinant on the possibly nonfree or infinite-dimensional functor X is taken. No finite-dimensionality, trace, field, projectivity, or flatness hypothesis is needed for this abstract step.

Let O be the ideal of R generated by all homogeneous odd elements. O is a nil ideal: each of its elements involves finitely many odd generators; supercommutativity and their square-zero relations make that finitely generated ideal nilpotent. R/O is an ordinary commutative ring. Modulo O, the determinant lemma for the nilpotent companion shift gives

    det S(z)=1-sum_(j=0)^(a-1) b_j z^(a-j)
               -sum_s (-1)^s c_s z^(a+1+s).         (D)

This is a UNIT polynomial, since S(z) is a polynomial matrix unit. Over a commutative ring every positive-degree coefficient of a unit polynomial is nilpotent. The powers occupied by the b_j and c_s are disjoint. Hence each b_j and each c_s is nilpotent modulo O, and therefore nilpotent in R itself (O is nil).

Only finitely many c_s occur because x is nilpotent. They and the b_j are homogeneous. A finite family of homogeneous, supercommuting nilpotents generates a nilpotent ideal. This conclusion remains valid with all independent exterior products retained.

Here is the coefficient recurrence. Put A_t=r u_t, B_(j,t)=t_j u_t and L_(s,t)=v x^s A_t. The initial values are A_0=0 and B_(j,0)=delta_j0. The displayed companion gives

    A_(t+1)=-x A_t+w B_(a-1,t),
    B_(j,t+1)=delta_j0 L_(0,t)
                +(if j>0 then B_(j-1,t) else 0)+b_j B_(a-1,t),
    L_(s,t+1)=-L_(s+1,t)+c_s B_(a-1,t).             (R-)

Every product is in the displayed order. Induction on t, simultaneously for all s, puts L_(s,t) in the ideal generated by the finitely many c_s; initially every L_(s,0) is zero. Thus every closed coefficient v_s r u_t belongs to the nilpotent ideal generated by the b_j and c_s.

Modulo that ideal, the B recurrence is just the nilpotent shift. Consequently B_(j,t)=delta_jt for t<a and B_(j,t)=0 for t≥a. This also proves the high-cup coordinate vanishing required by the separated-factor Schur calculation, rather than inferring it from feedback nilpotence alone.

## Zero-weight factor

If a=0, sigma is invertible and r=sigma^-1. Put w=r u. Then

    r y sigma=-x+w v.

The two endomorphisms 1+zx and 1-z(-x+w v) have polynomial inverses. The rectangular Jacobson identity (1-UV invertible iff 1-VU invertible) gives the polynomial unit

    p(z)=1-z v(1+zx)^-1 w
        =1-sum_s (-1)^s (v x^s w) z^(s+1)

in R[z]. Reduction modulo O and the unit-polynomial criterion imply that every c_s=v x^s w is nilpotent. For L_(s,t)=v x^s r u_t, the explicit recurrence is

    L_(s,0)=c_s,
    L_(s,t+1)=-L_(s+1,t)+c_s L_(0,t).

Induction on t puts every L_(s,t) in the nilpotent ideal generated by the finitely many c_s.

## Positive-weight factor: dual companion

Suppose instead Q=(sigma,v_0,...,v_(a-1)):X→Y⊕Id^a is the actual inversion, with inverse (r,k_0,...,k_(a-1)). Then

    Q x Q^-1 = [ -y       u e_0^T ]
                [ e_last w   J^T+e_last b^T ],
    w=v_a r,   b_j=v_a k_j.

The same Schur determinant has disjoint coefficient ranges for the b_j and c_s=w y^s u. Therefore those coefficients generate a nilpotent ideal. Define L_(s,t)=v x^s r y^t u and B_(s,j)=v x^s k_j. The dual companion gives

    L_(0,t)=0,
    L_(s+1,t)=-L_(s,t+1)+B_(s,a-1) c_t,
    B_(0,j)=delta_0j,
    B_(s+1,j)=L_(s,0) delta_j0
                +(if j>0 then B_(s,j-1) else 0)+B_(s,a-1) b_j. (R+)

Induction on s, simultaneously for all t, puts every L_(s,t) in the ideal generated by the c_t. Modulo the full ideal, B_(s,j)=delta_sj for s<a and vanishes for s≥a. The low-cap equality v_s r=0 for s<a also follows directly from the inverse identities. The signed Pi identifications with the categorical operators remain to be checked.

## Consequence for the tensor feedback

If the actual cross mates have the finite ordered expansions

    C=sum_r (signed) u_A,r v_B,-r-1,
    D=sum_s (signed) u_B,* v_A,s,

then D r_A C j_B is a finite sum of second-factor maps multiplied by first-factor closed coefficients v_A,s r_A u_A,r. Those coefficients belong to the nilpotent ideal above and supercommute past second-factor operators (with the prescribed parity-line signs). Hence D r_A C j_B is nilpotent, and

    (1-D r_A C j_B)^-1 = sum_(q=0)^(N-1) (D r_A C j_B)^q

for an ideal nilpotence exponent N. This proves ONLY the feedback pivot, conditional on the typed factor actions, dot nilpotence, actual cross-mate expansions, and signed companion comparisons. The remaining identity-summand Schur map, normalized VAC, and full nondegeneracy are not concluded.
