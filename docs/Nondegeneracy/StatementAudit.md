# Audit of the formal Nondegeneracy Conjecture

This note compares `SKM.NondegeneracyConjecture cs` (`OddMath/SKM/Nondegeneracy.lean`), and the
definitions it depends on, with the Nondegeneracy Conjecture of Brundan–Ellis, *Super Kac–Moody
2-categories*, arXiv:1701.04133v2, §8 (after Theorem 8.1). It also records what the end theorem
`QuiverHecke.nondegeneracy_of_symmetrizer` does and does not say. All references are to v2.

**Verdict.** The formal statement is faithful to the printed conjecture. It is at least as
strong as the printed conjecture. Where it differs, it is more general: it quantifies over every
choice of representatives and distinguished points, it allows arbitrary weight groups and
commutative ground rings, and it allows pairs of words whose weights agree in the weight group
rather than in the root lattice. No relation is missing, and no extra relation is imposed. One
point needs care: the normalizations `c_{λ;i}` are a supplied argument. They exist whenever the
simple roots are linearly independent over `ℤ`, which covers the paper's setting
(`SKM.CScalars.nonempty_of_linearIndependent`, below). For collapsed weight data they need not
exist.

## 1. Data and scalars

`SKM.Datum I X` is a Cartan datum on an additive group `X`, with simple roots `αᵢ` and simple
coroots `⟨hᵢ, -⟩`. Its Cartan matrix is a generalized Cartan matrix, and it carries a parity
`|i|` with `dᵢⱼ` even whenever `|i|` is odd. This is assumption (1.2).

The paper uses a complex vector space with linearly independent roots and coroots, the weight
lattice `P` and the root lattice `Q`. That is the special case in which `X = P` and the roots
are independent.

`SKM.Scalars` matches (1.3)–(1.4):

- units `tᵢⱼ` with `tᵢᵢ = 1`, and `tᵢⱼ = tⱼᵢ` when `dᵢⱼ = 0`;
- scalars `sᵢⱼ^{pq}` with `sᵢⱼ^{pq} = sⱼᵢ^{qp}`, vanishing when `p|i|` is odd;
- `2` a unit if some colour is odd.

The ground ring is an ordinary (purely even) commutative ring, as in the paper.
`Scalars.Homogeneous` (`SKM/Grading.lean`) is the homogeneity condition (1.31), stated on the
range `0 < p < dᵢⱼ`, `0 < q < dⱼᵢ` in which the scalars enter the relations. `Symmetrizer` is a
positive integer symmetrization `dᵢ dᵢⱼ = dⱼ dⱼᵢ`.

`SKM.CScalars Sc` is a family of units `c_{λ;i}` with the shift law
`c_{λ+αⱼ;i} = tᵢⱼ c_{λ;i}` of (1.17).

## 2. The 2-supercategory

`SKM.pres D Sc` (`SKM/Presentation.lean`) presents the paper's 2-supercategory as follows.

**Objects and generators.** Objects are weights with words of signed letters. The generators
are:

- dots, crossings, cups and caps with the parities of Definition 1.5;
- the leftward crossing of Definition 2.2;
- the ♦-cups and ♦-caps with `n` dots, which supply the entries of the inverse matrices in the
  invertibility axioms.

**Relations.** Each constructor of `Rel` was compared against the displays (1.7)–(1.14) and
(2.8)–(2.9), including orientation, dot position, the horizontal-composition convention and the
sign `(-1)^{|i|(|j|+s)}`:

- the quadratic relations, for equal colours and for distinct colours (the polynomial
  `Qᵢⱼ` with the `t` and `s` scalars);
- the dot-slide relations, including the extra term for equal colours;
- the braid relation, including the correction term for equal colours;
- the zigzag relations;
- the inverse relations for distinct colours, and the matrix identities (2.8)–(2.9) on both
  sides of the weight;
- the vanishing of ♦-cups and ♦-caps outside their range.

All match. Encoding invertibility by generators together with two-sided inverse identities is
equivalent to requiring the paper's 2-morphisms to be invertible, because two-sided inverses
are unique. The out-of-range ♦ generators are zero in the paper, by the conventions for these
entries, and they are zero here.

**Hom spaces.** Hom spaces are the free `k`-modules on layered diagrams modulo the span of
whiskered relation instances and super-interchange instances with the Koszul sign
(StringDiagrams `Presentation`). This is the quotient of free linear diagram spaces by the
2-sided ideal generated by the relations. No further relation is imposed. Parity homogeneity of
every relation is checked (`isParityHomogeneous`).

## 3. Bubbles, `SYM` and `β_λ`

**Bubbles.** `bubL` and `bubR` (`SKM/Bubbles.lean`) are the dotted bubbles of (2.13)–(2.16),
including the fake bubbles and the normalizations by `c_{λ;i}^{±1}`. `bubLs` and `bubRs` are the
shorthand (2.17), and `oddBubble` is (2.18).

**`SYM`.** `SYM` (`SKM/BubbleAlgebra.lean`) is the quotient of the free algebra on the following
generators:

- for every colour, generators `e_n`, which are even and central;
- for odd colours, an odd generator `d`;

modulo the relations that the `d` of distinct odd colours anticommute and that each `d` squares
to zero. This is the super tensor product of `Sym` over even colours and `Sym[d]` over odd
colours, as in the definition of `β_λ` after (1.20).

**`β_λ`.** `betaSYM` sends the generators to the paper's images:

- for even `i`, `e_n` goes to `c_{λ;i}^{-1}` times the left bubble with `n + *` dots, which is
  (1.19);
- for odd `i`, `e_n` goes to `c_{λ;i}^{-1}` times the left bubble with `2n + *` dots, and `d`
  goes to the odd bubble, which is (1.20).

The paper's `d` is `d e_0 = d h_0`, and both cases of `oddBubble` agree with the two formulas of
(1.20) for `d e_0` and `d h_0`. The images for small `n`, which involve fake bubbles, are those
of the paper.

Bijectivity of the expansion map (§5) is unchanged if `β_λ` is composed with a linear
automorphism of `SYM`. So the check only needs agreement up to such an automorphism. Agreement
on generators gives more than that.

## 4. Matchings and decorations

`MatchingBasisChoice a b` is a choice, before Theorem 8.1, of the following data:

- `members`: a set of `(a, b)`-matchings (`IsMatching₂`), given as generic layerings of cups,
  crossings and caps. These are reduced in the paper's sense (`StrictReduced₂`): no strand
  crosses itself, two strands cross at most once, and each strand has at most one critical
  point;
- one representative per pairing (`unique`, `complete`; pairings are compared by `Iso` of
  `pairing₂`);
- `points`: a distinguished point on every strand (`OnStrand₂`). Points lie at heights between
  layers, so they are away from crossings and critical points;
- `serialize` and `decorates`: for every function from strands to natural numbers, a decorated
  diagram with that many dots at each distinguished point (`Decorates₂`).

`layers` is the diagram, and `xev` is `f(σ, λ)`.

The paper fixes one choice. `NondegeneracyConjecture` quantifies over all choices, and
`MatchingBasisChoice.nonempty` shows choices exist, so the quantifier is not empty.

Matchings exist only when `wt(a) = wt(b)` in the root lattice. The formal hypothesis is
equality in `X`, which can be weaker when the roots are dependent in `X`. For the additional
pairs there are no matchings, and Theorem 8.1 (`expansion_surjective`) gives a zero Hom space.
The basis statement is then the statement that the zero module is free on the empty set. This
is a harmless generalization; in the paper's setting the two conditions coincide.

## 5. Free right `SYM`-module

`IsBasis` is bijectivity of `expansion cs μ : (Labels →₀ SYM) → Hom(E_a 1_λ, E_b 1_λ)`. This
map sends `p` at a decorated matching `σ` to `β_λ(p)`, placed in the rightmost region
(`bubAt`, the horizontal composite on the right), followed by `f(σ, λ)`.

**Action.** Endomorphisms of `1_λ` act through the rightmost region. By super interchange,
placing `β_λ(p)` below or above `f(σ, λ)` differs only by the sign `(-1)^{|σ||p|}`, and this sign
is again an automorphism of `SYM` applied coefficientwise. So bijectivity of `expansion` is
exactly the paper's assertion: `Hom` is free as a right `SYM`-module with basis
`{f(σ, λ)}`.

**Surjectivity** is Theorem 8.1 (`expansion_surjective`).

## 6. Normalizations

`CScalars Sc` is a supplied argument of the end theorem. The paper chooses the `c_{λ;i}`, and a
choice exists in its setting.

`SKM.CScalars.nonempty_of_linearIndependent` (`SKM/CScalarsExistence.lean`) shows existence
whenever the `αᵢ` are `ℤ`-linearly independent in `X`, for arbitrary `tᵢⱼ`:

1. choose a representative `r(λ)` of `λ` modulo the root lattice;
2. write `λ - r(λ) = Σ nⱼ αⱼ`;
3. set `c_{λ;i} = Π tᵢⱼ^{nⱼ}`.

`QuiverHecke.exists_nondegeneracy_of_linearIndependent` combines this with the end theorem.

For dependent roots a choice can fail to exist. For example, if `α₀ + α₁ = 0`, the shift law
forces `tᵢ₀ tᵢ₁ = 1`. For such scalars the end theorem says nothing. The data involved are
outside the paper's setting.

## 7. The end theorem

`nondegeneracy_of_symmetrizer` has the following hypotheses:

- `I` and `X` in one universe;
- a symmetrizer;
- `IsUnit (2 : R)`;
- homogeneous scalars;
- `cs`.

Its conclusion is `NondegeneracyConjecture cs`. It holds for every set of colours, finite or
not, and every commutative ring.

**Universe condition.** The requirement that `I` and `X` share a universe costs nothing, since
`ULift` changes the universe of either type without changing the mathematics.

**Hypotheses.** The proof chain enters through `nondegeneracy_of_rightVacuumEntrySupply` with
the term `rightVacuumEntrySupply`, which is proved. No hypothesis of the end theorem restates
part of the conclusion. The `#print axioms` check in `scripts/NonVacuity/SwappedRouteNondegeneracy.lean`
reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

**Non-vacuity.** The end theorem has two concrete applications:

- `scripts/NonVacuity/SwappedRouteNondegeneracy.lean`: a mixed even/odd datum with trivial
  scalars;
- `scripts/NonVacuity/NondegeneracyIndependentRoots.lean`: the same datum with `t₀₁ = t₁₀ = 2`,
  and a purely even datum with `t₀₁ = 2`, `t₁₀ = 3` and `s₀₁^{11} = 5`. In both cases the
  normalizations are constructed rather than supplied.

## 8. Even specialization

The presentation is uniform in the parity. At parity identically zero:

- every super sign is `1`;
- the ♦ generators and relations are those of Definition 1.5 and Definition 2.2 for even data;
- the odd bubble and the `d` factors do not occur, and `SYM` is the tensor product of copies of
  `Sym`.

So `pres D Sc` with `D` purely even is the paper's even Kac–Moody 2-category with general
scalars `t` and `s` and normalizations `c`. The end theorem therefore includes even
nondegeneracy, for homogeneous scalars, over every commutative ring with `2` invertible and for
any set of colours.

The checked statement relating this to the literature is `EvenBridge.nondegeneracyConjecture_of_isEven`.
It covers Khovanov–Lauda scalars (`t` as in KL III, `s = 0`), every commutative ring including
those in which `2` is not a unit, and finitely many colours. It proves the conclusion
independently of the odd route, using KL III's basis theorem.

The following remain outside what is checked:

- even nondegeneracy for general homogeneous `t`, `s` when `2` is not a unit. Examples are
  rings such as `ℤ`, which Webster arXiv:1603.06311v4 Theorem 3.13 (invertible symmetrizers) and
  Brundan–Wang–Webster arXiv:2505.22929v2 Theorem 5.6 (graded coefficient rings) cover;
- the graded-coefficient, non-homogeneous parameter range of Brundan–Wang–Webster (Phase 2);
- a formal identification of `pres D Sc` at parity zero with a separately formalized general-
  parameter even 2-category. Only the Khovanov–Lauda presentation exists in the dependencies,
  and the comparison with it is the 2-functor of `EvenBridge`;
- normalizations for benchmark realizations whose roots are dependent in the weight group.
  Brundan–Wang–Webster assume independent coroots, not independent roots.
