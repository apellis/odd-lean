# Finite coefficient reservoirs and positive closures

**Status: written proof, not a Lean theorem.** This supplies the fixed-content
boundedness hypothesis of `FusionReservoirLemma.md` §2 for the graded full-ELV
action of `DeformedGradedAction.md`. It uses the finite right-generation result
of `DeformedSquareResolution.md`, including its monic annihilator. It does not
identify the fused feedback or prove the residual identity-summand matrix invertible.

## 1. Hypotheses and an explicit coefficient quotient

Fix finite compatible colour support, a symmetrizable Cartan superdatum with
positive integral symmetrizers d_i, and a purely even field k with 2 invertible.
Retain the prescribed homogeneous Q parameters and their invertible leading
coefficients. Retain the weight realization and cutoff vector N_i of the
action: weights are λ=−Λ+β, with the required coroot pairings. If Λ needs an
extended weight lattice, use that realization and its restriction; no existence
of Λ in an arbitrary original lattice is inferred here. Contents β have finite
nonnegative multiplicities. Distinct contents can have the same weight.

Use exactly the coefficient algebra A in `DeformedIdealSum.md` §1. Write δ_i
for its odd generator, not for the symmetrizer. For odd i the polynomial and
coefficient ring are

    N_i=2m>0: C_i(x²)−xδ_i(C_i(x²)−χ_(i,m))/x²,
              k[χ_(i,1),…,χ_(i,m)]⊗Λ(δ_i)/(δ_iχ_(i,m));
    N_i=2m+1: (x−δ_i)C_i(x²),
              k[χ_(i,1),…,χ_(i,m)]⊗Λ(δ_i);
    N_i=0:   1, with no δ_i.

Even-colour coefficients are even polynomial generators. Take the super tensor
product over all supported colours. All independent odd generators, their
cross-colour exterior products, and the even-cutoff ELV relations remain in
this presentation. In particular, do not identify odd generators of different
colours, invert a top coefficient, or omit δ_iχ_(i,m)=0.

List the finitely many positive-degree even coefficient generators as
c_1,…,c_t. For any chosen integers L_a≥1 set

    T=(c_1^(L_1),…,c_t^(L_t)) ⊂ A,     Abar=A/T.

The ideal is homogeneous for both gradings. The integer degrees are those of
`DeformedGradedAction.md`: deg δ_i=2d_i, deg χ_(i,r)=4d_i r,
and deg c_(i,r)=2d_i r for an even colour. Degree modulo two is NOT parity.
If t=0, use T=0. Monomials c_1^a_1⋯c_t^a_t δ_(j_1)⋯δ_(j_b), with
0≤a_a<L_a and distinct increasing odd indices, span Abar. There are finitely
many, and the ELV relations only take a further quotient. Thus Abar is a
finite-dimensional bigraded k-algebra. This does not presume exterior
flatness. The truncation is of deformation coefficients, not of the leading
units in k or of the Q parameters. No finite-degree separation assertion is
part of this construction.

## 2. Specialization is the defined cyclotomic quotient

Let R_β=A⊗ˢR(β), I_β its two-sided first-position cyclotomic ideal, and
B_β=R_β/I_β, with B_0=A (there is no first-position relation at height zero).
Define Rbar_β and Bbar_β by the same presentations with coefficients in Abar.
Then there are canonical bigraded algebra isomorphisms

    Rbar_β ≅ R_β/TR_β,
    Bbar_β ≅ R_β/(I_β+TR_β) ≅ Abar⊗_A B_β.          (BC)

Here the tensor product uses the supercentral coefficient structure; since T
is generated by even central elements, it is also the ordinary quotient by
TB_β. To prove (BC), send each coefficient, word idempotent, dot and crossing
to its namesake. The images satisfy precisely the specialized relations.
The reverse maps from the presented algebra send the same generators back;
the two composites fix every generator. The image of I_β is the ideal
generated by the specialized cyclotomic relations, since a generator of its
image is a finite sum of left and right multiples of such relations. This
proves the quotient assertion without tensor exactness on the left.

The same argument identifies corners and balanced tensor kernels. For example,
for γ=β+α_i and e=e(β,i),

    Abar⊗_A (B_γ e) ≅ Bbar_γ e,
    Abar⊗_A (P⊗_(B_β) Q)
       ≅ (Abar⊗_A P)⊗_(Bbar_β)(Abar⊗_A Q).          (BT)

These maps and inverses are induced by pure tensors and multiplication of
coefficients, with the super tensor signs. Balancing gives both inverse
identities. Equivalently both sides are the same quotient of the tensor
presentation by T; hence neither formula asserts preservation of a kernel of
an arbitrary linear map. Parity twists preserve T and descend unchanged.

In particular all prescribed kernel maps of the action specialize through
(BT). Their inverse matrices specialize as well: tensoring the two identities
ρρ⁻¹=1 and ρ⁻¹ρ=1 preserves them. Coefficient-linear homogeneous maps preserve
T times their modules. Thus the standard graded action, including its
homogeneous principal inverse blocks, exists over Abar with exactly these
quotient algebras. We do not infer injectivity in a specialized resolution
by flatness. For finite generation below, only its already proved finite
right-generation consequence is specialized.

## 3. Finite dimension at every fixed content

For γ=β+α_i let F_(β,i)=B_γ e(β,i), the induction kernel, viewed as a
LEFT B_γ, RIGHT B_β bimodule. `DeformedSquareResolution.md` §§2–5 proves
that it is finite projective on the RIGHT. The finite qualification matters:
its preliminary PBW modules are finite projective over B_β[s], not over B_β.
The cokernel F is finite over B_β[s] and killed by a central monic a(s).
If f_1,…,f_u generate it over B_β[s] and deg a=m, then

    f_j s^v,  1≤j≤u, 0≤v<m

span over B_β by monic reduction. If m=0 the kernel is zero. This is the
finite-generation input used here; projectivity alone would not suffice.
Tensor a finite generating surjection with Abar, or specialize its generating
expressions. By (BC)–(BT), Bbar_γ e(β,i) is therefore finitely generated as a
RIGHT Bbar_β-module. No left-projectivity theorem is needed.

Induct on height |γ|. At height zero Bbar_0=Abar is finite-dimensional.
For positive height, the endpoint idempotents give

    1_(Bbar_γ)=Σ_(i:γ_i>0) e(γ−α_i,i),
    Bbar_γ=⊕_(i:γ_i>0) Bbar_γ e(γ−α_i,i)             (FD)

as k-vector spaces (and left Bbar_γ-modules). Each endpoint is itself the
sum of the finitely many word idempotents with that last colour. Orthogonality
proves the direct sum; no idempotent is assumed nonzero. Its summand is
finite over the predecessor on the RIGHT, so a finite right generating
set times a k-basis of Bbar_(γ−α_i) spans it over k. The induction hypothesis
and finite sum (FD) prove dim_k Bbar_γ<∞. This includes zero corners and
zero cutoff polynomials. The argument never interchanges left and right
module structures.

Since Bbar_γ is a direct-sum integer-graded finite-dimensional vector space,
its integer support is finite: choose a finite basis, decompose its vectors
into finitely many homogeneous components, and take their union of supports.
Thus it has an upper degree bound M_γ. Negative crossing degrees are allowed;
no assertion that the cyclotomic algebra is nonnegatively graded is used.

A finitely generated graded Bbar_β-module is finite-dimensional over k and
has finite total degree support by the same argument. Induction sends such a
module to Bbar_γ e⊗_(Bbar_β) M, a quotient of a tensor product of finite
k-vector spaces. Restriction sends it to eM, then applies the prescribed
integer/parity shift. Both are finite-dimensional. Induction on word length
therefore proves the same for every finite E/F word on a finite module, and
for finite direct sums of such outputs. This does NOT claim arbitrary
modules or Hom spaces in the all-module target are finite-dimensional.

## 4. Natural endomorphisms and the supercentre

Use the enriched graded supermodule category at one fixed content β: all
homogeneous maps of arbitrary integer degree and parity are available, with
finite sums of bidegrees. For a homogeneous map of parity p the convention is
f(bm)=(-1)^(p|b|)b f(m). Naturality of a parity-p identity transformation η
against a parity-q map g is η_N g=(-1)^(pq)g η_M.

Evaluation at 1 on the regular module identifies its homogeneous natural
endomorphisms with the homogeneous supercentre of B=Bbar_β:

    Z_s(B)={z homogeneous : zb=(-1)^(|z||b|)bz for homogeneous b}.

Here is the identification, including its sign. Put z=η_B(1). For homogeneous
m∈M the map f_m:B→M, b↦(-1)^(|m||b|)bm has parity |m| and integer degree
deg m. Evaluating naturality at 1 gives

    η_M(m)=(-1)^(p|m|) f_m(z)=z m.

Taking M=B and m=b, signed B-linearity of η_B gives
zb=(-1)^(p|b|)bz. Conversely this relation makes left multiplication by z
signed B-linear on every module and supernatural against every homogeneous
map; composition is multiplication of z's in the same order. The integer
degree is preserved because 1 has degree zero. The regular module and all
its needed shifts belong even to the finite-module subcategory. This proof
would not justify the claim for a category with only degree-zero test maps
and no shift-compatible enrichment. It identifies the supercentre, NOT the
ordinary centre of the underlying associative algebra.

Consequently the algebra of homogeneous identity transformations at β is a
graded subalgebra of the finite-dimensional Bbar_β and has upper support bound
M_β. Its bound is valid for the action on ALL modules, including infinite ones:
a zero supercentral element acts as zero there. Hom-finiteness is unnecessary.

## 5. Joint nilpotence of the prescribed closures

Fix β and a colour i. Write R_i for the principal inverse block in the actual
all-height Rouquier matrix; this avoids confusion with a dot index r.
The graded action proves deg R_i=0, after including its specified parity
transports. For the dotted cup and cap at h=h_i(−Λ+β), it proves

    deg u_r=d_i(1+h)+2d_i r,
    deg v_s=d_i(1−h)+2d_i s.

The closure of `FusionReservoirLemma.md` is the typed endomorphism

    b_(i;s,r)=v_s R_i u_r,     deg b_(i;s,r)=2d_i(r+s+1)>0.   (CL)

This uses the principal inverse block, not an inverse of the bare crossing.
The supercentre identification and bound above apply to (CL). Every product
of n such closures at this fixed content, even with varying colours and dot
indices, has degree at least 2n, because d_i≥1. It therefore vanishes when
2n>M_β. One can choose any positive integer n satisfying this inequality;
no numeric bound on M_β is required. This is joint nilpotence of the whole
positive closure family, not just nilpotence of each element. It also proves
nilpotence of their action on any finite reservoir component from §3.

For a finite content window W take the maximum of the finitely many M_β.
This supplies a common exponent for closures acting componentwise on W.
For closures whiskered along a specified finite word on a specified finite
module, there are only finitely many visited contents, shifts and finite
modules; their supports give the corresponding finite bounds. These facts
do not supply a bound on every content with a given weight: that fiber can
be infinite. On the product weight category, an operator preserving content
and having these nilpotent components can instead be inverted componentwise:
(1−T_β)⁻¹=Σ_(0≤j<n_β) T_β^j. If each T_β has degree and parity zero, these
inverses assemble to one homogeneous degree-zero natural map, even when the
n_β are unbounded. There is no single finite geometric series globally.
For an operator mixing contents, such an assembly requires a separate
finite-window/local-finiteness argument and is not asserted here.

Finally, suppose a SPECIFIED feedback on a reservoir/probe tensor component
is a finite sum of tensors of these closures with homogeneous probe maps.
In every term of a high power, super tensor interchange groups the reservoir
factors, with sign (-1)^(|probe||reservoir|) at each exchange. Their product
vanishes by the common exponent, so that specified feedback is nilpotent.
The probe may be integer-ungraded, localized, infinite-dimensional and have
nonnilpotent dots. Only its parity and the tensor interchange law are used;
this is not ordinary scalar centrality and imposes no finite-probe condition.

## 6. Boundaries of the conclusion

The fixed-content boundedness and closure nilpotence hypotheses are now
supplied for these finite-coefficient quotients of the graded ELV factor
action. Still required are identification of the genuine fused feedback
with the specified separated expression, its tensor-factor support and signs,
and invertibility of the all-height residual identity-summand matrix.
Transport through a Hom/precomposition construction may require the missing
factor left adjunction; it is not obtained by calling Hom finite-dimensional.
Neither that transport nor the residual Schur comparison is proved here.

A separation argument must additionally show that sufficiently large
homogeneous coefficient truncations preserve the nonzero coefficient being
detected, together with the required cutoff/content quantifiers. No such
cofinal separation statement, normalized bubble-coordinate theorem, fused
action, or nondegeneracy theorem follows merely from the bound above.
